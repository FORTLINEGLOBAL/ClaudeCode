# The Two-Sentence Claim

From *Crossing the Chasm*, ch. 6. The claim must pass the elevator test: explainable in the time
it takes to ride up an elevator, and repeatable word-of-mouth by someone who heard it once.

## The formula

```
For                        (target customers — beachhead segment ONLY)
Who are dissatisfied with  (the current MARKET ALTERNATIVE)
Our product is a           (product category)
That provides              (the compelling reason to buy)

Unlike                     (the PRODUCT ALTERNATIVE)
We have assembled          (key whole product features for this specific application)
```

Sentence one = who for / what for. Sentence two = differentiation, and it is the one teams drop.
Always reinforce sentence two.

## Rules

1. **Line 1 names the beachhead, not the ambition.** If it reads "enterprises,"
   "mid-market companies," or "developers," it is wrong. It should name a role in a situation.
2. **Line 2 must be a real incumbent** — the thing the budget goes to today. Not "spreadsheets
   and manual processes" unless that genuinely is the funded alternative.
3. **Line 3 is a category the buyer already recognizes.** Inventing a category here is how
   pragmatists get lost. Invent the category later, from a position of leadership.
4. **Line 4 is an economic consequence, not a feature.** "Cuts close from eleven days to three,"
   not "provides automated reconciliation."
5. **Line 5 must be a credible peer**, not a strawman. If you cannot name a product alternative,
   the position is probably wrong — go back to the compass.
6. **Line 6 is the whole product for this segment**, which is why it can only be written after
   the doughnut exists.
7. **This is a positioning statement, not a tagline.** Taglines derive from it. Do not ship it
   as copy.

## Worked examples

**Verinata** (from the book):
> For older pregnant mothers and others who want an alternative to amniocentesis to screen for
> Down syndrome, Verinata provides a genetic analysis of fetal DNA that does not involve
> inserting a needle into the womb.
> Unlike other genetic tests for fetal abnormalities, the Verinata test is the most accurate on
> the market.

**Box** (constructed from the book's discussion):
> For IT and line-of-business leaders in regulated enterprises who are dissatisfied with
> SharePoint's on-premise content collaboration, Box is a cloud content management platform
> that lets teams share and govern documents with external parties without a VPN or an IT ticket.
> Unlike Dropbox, we have assembled enterprise administration, audit trails, retention policy,
> and the compliance certifications that a regulated enterprise's security review requires.

**Workday** (constructed from the book's discussion):
> For HR executives at large enterprises who are dissatisfied with the cost and rigidity of their
> PeopleSoft installation, Workday is a human capital management system that lets them
> reorganize, report, and run payroll without a multi-year upgrade project.
> Unlike Salesforce.com and other SaaS platforms built for a different function, we have
> assembled a complete HCM suite with the data model, localization, and integration set an
> enterprise HR organization actually runs on.

## Elevator test protocol

1. Read the claim aloud, timed. Over ~30 seconds, cut.
2. Have someone who has not seen it repeat it back an hour later. What survives is your real
   claim.
3. Check against the five failure costs: can it travel by word of mouth? Will it govern every
   piece of marketing collateral? Will it point R&D at one target? Can a partner tell what
   you are committing to? Would an experienced investor call it an investable strategy?

## Evidence to pair with the claim

A claim to leadership is meaningless if it can be disputed. For pragmatists, the evidence that
counts is, in order:

1. Named customers **in the beachhead segment** who will take a reference call.
2. Whole product completeness — visible, with named partners on the outer wedges.
3. Analysts and trade press **for that vertical**, not general technology press.
4. Partner commitment (a systems integrator staffing a practice around you is worth more than
   any award).
5. Company durability signals: funding, longevity, support SLAs.
